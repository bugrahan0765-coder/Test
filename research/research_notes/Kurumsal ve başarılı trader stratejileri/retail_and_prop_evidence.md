# Retail / individual day trader and prop-firm trader performance: hard evidence

Notes compiled 2026-10-11. Evidence grades used below: **[PR]** = peer-reviewed paper; **[WP]** = working paper; **[REG]** = regulator data; **[CO]** = company self-reported (marketing/disclosure, unaudited unless stated); **[PRESS]** = trade press / third-party estimate; **[2ND]** = secondary summary of a paper (not verified against the original text).

## Q1. Barber, Lee, Liu, Odean: Taiwan day traders (JFM 2014; "Do day traders rationally learn about their ability?" / "Learning Fast or Slow", RAPS 2020)

### Takeaway
In the complete Taiwan Stock Exchange record (1992-2006, ~450,000 day traders per year), only about 17-20% make money net of fees in a given year, and fewer than 1% (about 1,000-4,000 people per year) are *predictably* profitable. Skill does exist and persists strongly at the very top (top 500 earn 37.9 bps/day net), but it is extremely rare. Winners trade more, concentrate on a few stocks, and mostly use aggressive (liquidity-taking) orders; liquidity provision is not their main source of profit.

### Cited Findings
- [PR] Sample: complete transaction data of the Taiwan Stock Exchange 1992-2006. Day trading = 17% of all TSE volume, stable over the 15 years; individuals account for 95% of day trading. — [Barber, Lee, Liu, Odean (2014), "The cross-section of speculator skill: Evidence from day trading", Journal of Financial Markets 18, 1-24 (author PDF)](https://faculty.haas.berkeley.edu/odean/papers/day%20traders/The%20Cross-Section%20of%20Speculator%20Skill.pdf)
- [PR] About 450,000 Taiwanese individuals day trade in an average year; 277,000 trade > NT$600,000/yr (~US$20k). About 20% of these earn positive abnormal returns net of fees in a year (17-20% depending on population definition). — [same paper](https://faculty.haas.berkeley.edu/odean/papers/day%20traders/The%20Cross-Section%20of%20Speculator%20Skill.pdf)
- [PR] Abstract: "Less than 1% of the day trader population is able to predictably and reliably earn positive abnormal returns net of fees." Only the ~4,000 most profitable from the prior year (out of ~450,000) earn reliably positive net abnormal returns the next year; with the statutory max commission (14.25 bps one-way) the number of predictably profitable traders falls to ~1,000. — [same paper](https://faculty.haas.berkeley.edu/odean/papers/day%20traders/The%20Cross-Section%20of%20Speculator%20Skill.pdf)
- [PR] Persistence: ranking on prior-year Sharpe ratio (min. 10 day-trading days), the top 500 earn **61.3 bps/day gross and 37.9 bps/day net** (t = 96 and 61) on their day-trading portfolio in the following year (24.1 bps/day net under 14.25 bps commission). Bottom-ranked/loss-history traders earn -11.5 bps/day gross and -28.9 bps/day net. Top-500 mean ranking-period Sharpe ratio (daily) = 0.66. Top-to-bottom gross spread = 73 bps/day, vs ~5 bps/day in Coval et al. (US) and 4.4 bps/day in Grinblatt et al. (Finland). — [same paper](https://faculty.haas.berkeley.edu/odean/papers/day%20traders/The%20Cross-Section%20of%20Speculator%20Skill.pdf)
- [PR] Win/lose persistence contingency table: 20.0% winners in year y; 6.6% are winners in both y and y+1 versus 3.9% expected by chance (Panel B, heavier traders: 6.2% vs 3.1% expected). Sample of 3.8 million trader-year pairs. — [same paper, Table 3](https://faculty.haas.berkeley.edu/odean/papers/day%20traders/The%20Cross-Section%20of%20Speculator%20Skill.pdf)
- [PR] Even the 500 *most active* day traders lose money net of costs (net alpha -7.4 bps/day at 5 bps commission; -20.9 bps at 14.25 bps), although their gross intraday alpha is +14.4 bps/day; activity raises gross skill, not net profit. ~10% of all day trading is from these 500 traders. — [same paper](https://faculty.haas.berkeley.edu/odean/papers/day%20traders/The%20Cross-Section%20of%20Speculator%20Skill.pdf)
- [PR] Order type: about 70% of individual investors' trades come from aggressive (marketable) limit orders; day trader profit groups range 64-78% aggressive; the top 500 are slightly less aggressive (**64% aggressive**, i.e. "nearly two-thirds" of their trades still take liquidity). The most passive decile (16% aggressive) earns only +3.8 bps/day gross and loses after costs; **all aggressiveness deciles lose money after costs**. Authors: "providing liquidity does not appear to be the major source of day trading profits." — [same paper, Sec. 5.2, Table 6](https://faculty.haas.berkeley.edu/odean/papers/day%20traders/The%20Cross-Section%20of%20Speculator%20Skill.pdf)
- [PR] Sources of profit: profitable day traders earn more in hard-to-value stocks (small, volatile) and around earnings announcements, consistent with private information or faster processing of public information; losers lose in the same situations. — [same paper](https://faculty.haas.berkeley.edu/odean/papers/day%20traders/The%20Cross-Section%20of%20Speculator%20Skill.pdf)
- [PR] Logistic model of P(profitable): past performance (Sharpe of past returns/profits) is "by a large margin" the best predictor (probability impact 7.3% and 2.8% for a 25th->75th percentile move); next is concentration of trading in a few stocks (1.2%); experience, volume, short selling and aggressiveness add little (0.1-0.5%). "It takes a truly remarkable profile to garner an even 50-50 chance of being profitable." — [same paper, Table 7](https://faculty.haas.berkeley.edu/odean/papers/day%20traders/The%20Cross-Section%20of%20Speculator%20Skill.pdf)
- [WP] Learning paper (Oct 2017 version): "more than 75% of all day traders quit within two years"; poor performers are more likely to quit, but unprofitable experienced traders persist at almost the same rate as profitable ones. Fraction of profitable (lifetime net positive, >=10 days experience) day traders "consistently about 5%"; unprofitable traders account for 72% of day-trading volume overall and ~80% in the final years. Predictably profitable traders generate only 9.81% of day-trading volume and are "less than 3% of all day traders on an average day". Profitable traders with >40 day-trading days in the past year earn more than 40 bps round-trip costs, mostly on the day of the trade. — [Barber, Lee, Liu, Odean, Zhang, "Do Day Traders Rationally Learn About Their Ability?" (WP, Oct 2017)](https://faculty.haas.berkeley.edu/odean/papers/day%20traders/day%20trading%20and%20learning%20110217.pdf)
- [PR][2ND] Published version (Review of Asset Pricing Studies 10(1), 2020, 61-93, retitled "Learning Fast or Slow?"): aggregate day-trader performance negative; 74% of day trading volume is from traders with a history of losses; 97% of day traders are likely to lose money in future day trading. — [IDEAS/RePEc abstract](https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/rasset/v10y2020i1p61-93..html)
- [2ND] Quit figures widely quoted (80% quit within 2 years; 13% still trading after 3 years; 7% after 5 years) come from a forum quote of the paper; only the "more than 75% within two years" figure was verified in the 2017 PDF. — [Elite Trader thread](https://elitetrader.com/et/threads/do-day-traders-rationally-learn-about-their-ability.318301/)

### Inferences
- The Taiwan evidence is the strongest proof that intraday skill *exists* (the top-500 net alpha t-stat of 61 cannot be luck) but that it sits in well under 1% of participants, and is identified mainly by a long, statistically strong track record.
- The ~20% "profitable in a given year" vs <1% "predictably profitable" gap is a direct analogue of prop challenges: a one-off pass is weak evidence of skill; repeated passes/payouts are the signal.
- Taiwan winners were stock pickers exploiting information asymmetry in specific names; this mechanism transfers poorly to index CFDs/gold where single-name information edges do not exist.

### Gaps
- No breakdown of holding time in minutes for the top group (only "most profit earned on the day of the trade").
- The 2020 published-version numbers (74%, 97%) are from the abstract via IDEAS; full text not read.

## Q2. Chague, De-Losso, Giovannetti: Brazil mini-index futures ("Day Trading for a Living?") and related Brazilian evidence

### Takeaway
Of everyone who started day trading Brazilian mini-Ibovespa futures in 2013-2015 and persisted at least 300 days, 97% lost money; only ~1.1% earned more than the minimum wage and ~0.5% more than a bank teller's starting salary, and there was no evidence of learning with experience.

### Cited Findings
- [WP] Sample: 19,646 individuals who began day trading mini-index (WIN) futures 2013-2015 (data 2012-2017); 1,551 (7.9%) persisted more than 300 trading days; 50.8% traded only 2-50 days. Of the 1,551 persistent traders, **97% lost money** net of fees; **1.1% (17 people) earned more than the Brazilian minimum wage (US$16/day)**; **0.5% (8 people) earned more than a bank teller's initial salary (US$54/day)**; the best trader averaged US$310/day with a daily standard deviation of US$2,560. Learning regression on 714,637 trader-days: no improvement with experience (coefficient -0.019). — [Tradicted summary of Chague, De-Losso & Giovannetti (2020), SSRN WP](https://www.tradicted.com/research/chagu-day-2020/); paper record: [IDEAS/RePEc FGV EESP WP 525](https://ideas.repec.org/p/fgv/eesptd/525.html); [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/334630772_Day_Trading_for_a_Living)
- [2ND] Conflict: another summary gives 0.4% (not 0.5%) above the bank-teller benchmark. — [search-result summary of Semantic Scholar record](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Day-Trading-for-a-Living-Chague-De-Losso/15a142e2b9ec2c44d09c556b972274625c537d24)
- [2ND] Authors conclude it is virtually impossible for individuals to compete with HFTs and day trade for a living. — [Semantic Scholar record](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Day-Trading-for-a-Living-Chague-De-Losso/15a142e2b9ec2c44d09c556b972274625c537d24)
- [PRESS] Giovannetti's broader FGV dataset of individual day trades in WDO (mini-dollar) and WIN (mini-index) futures 2012-2017: about 91% of monitored day traders lost money; cumulative loss R$68.4 million counting only B3 fees, avg R$35.90 per investor per day. — [Bem Paraná](https://www.bemparana.com.br/tecnologia/apenas-8-dos-day-traders-atuam-por-mais-de-300-dias-275043/); [InvestNews](https://investnews.com.br/?p=16793); [Suno](https://www.suno.com.br/noticias/?p=243567)

### Inferences
- Brazilian mini-index futures are the closest academic analogue to retail intraday index trading (cheap, leveraged, electronic, HFT-populated). Survival of 300+ days does not imply skill: 97% of survivors still lose.
- The "minimum wage" framing matters for prop economics: even the few winners earned small daily amounts with huge variance (SD ~8x the mean for the best trader).

### Gaps
- Could not access the primary PDF (ResearchGate 403); numbers are from a detailed secondary summary.
- No separate mini-dollar-only loss percentage was found.

## Q3. Other academic evidence: US, Taiwan futures, Korea, India, persistence, leverage

### Takeaway
Every large dataset finds a majority of retail day traders losing (typically 70-97%), a small persistent winning tail, and that leverage and overconfidence magnify losses. Persistence of individual skill is real but weak in broad samples (Coval et al.: cross-period correlation ~10%).

### Cited Findings
- [PR] Jordan & Diltz (2003), Financial Analysts Journal 59(6), 85-94: about twice as many day traders lose money as make money; roughly a fifth were more than marginally profitable; profitability tracked Nasdaq Composite moves. — [CFA Institute](https://rpc.cfainstitute.org/research/financial-analysts-journal/2003/the-profitability-of-day-traders); [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/taf/ufajxx/v59y2003i6p85-94.html). (Sample often quoted as 334 accounts, Feb 1998-Oct 1999 — unverified.)
- [WP][2ND] Garvey & Murphy, "The Profitability of Active Stock Traders": 1,386 day traders at a US direct-access broker, Mar-Jun 2000; about half profitable after commissions; most trades were limit orders; trades against market makers were less profitable, trades via ECNs more profitable; skill vs luck not separable. — [CXO Advisory summary](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/individual-investing/the-odds-of-winning-as-an-active-trader/); [Murphy, Oxford PDF "Active Traders"](https://www.nuff.ox.ac.uk/Users/MurphyA/Active%20Traders.pdf)
- [WP] Garvey & Murphy, "How Profitable Day Traders Trade" (96,323 trades of a US proprietary team): profitable traders were more active when liquidity traders were present and often updated quotes ahead of market makers. — [conference abstract](https://content.csbs.utah.edu/~ehrbar/erc2002/abstracts/A407.html)
- [PR] Garvey & Murphy (2004), FAJ, "Are Professional Traders Too Slow to Realize Their Losses?": US prop stock-trading team made >$1.4M intraday profits but showed the disposition effect (held losers too long); cutting losers sooner and holding winners longer would have raised profits. — [CFA Institute](https://rpc.cfainstitute.org/research/financial-analysts-journal/2004/are-professional-traders-too-slow-to-realize-their-losses)
- [PR] Kuo & Lin (2013), Journal of Banking & Finance 37, 3548-3561: 3,470 individual day traders on the Taiwan Futures Exchange (Oct 2007-Sep 2008) lost on average NT$61,500 after costs (NT$26,700 before costs); traders are overconfident and misinterpret information; excessive trading hurts overconfident losers but not winners; more experienced traders trade more aggressively without gaining skill. — [HKU repository PDF](https://hub.hku.hk/bitstream/10722/184789/1/content.pdf); [NCCU record](https://commerce.nccu.edu.tw/en/faculty/facultylist/writing_journal/Kuo-Wei-Yu-Tse-Chun-Lin-2013-Overconfident-Individual-Day-Traders-Evidence-from-the-Taiwan-Futures-Market-Journal-of-Banking-and-Finance-Vol-37-pp-3548-3561-SSCI-%E7%82%BA%E9%80%9A%E8%A8%8A%E4%BD%9C%E8%80%85-206080)
- [2ND] Same study: median net profit per trader about -NT$30,000 (~-US$940); fewer than 20% of individual futures day traders make a positive net profit. — [CXO Advisory](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/individual-investing/performance-of-futures-day-traders/)
- [PR] Korea, KOSPI 200 futures (Investment Analysts Journal 41, 2012, "The profitability of day trading: an empirical study using high-quality data"): domestic individuals incur substantial day-trading losses; heavier and more frequent day traders are more likely to lose. A 2024 Journal of Futures Markets paper (2010-2023 data) finds domestic individuals lose more in bearish and low-volatility markets. — [SKKU record](https://pure.skku.edu/en/publications/the-profitability-of-day-trading-an-empirical-study-using-high-qu/); [XJTLU record (frequent trading, KOSPI 200)](https://scholar.xjtlu.edu.cn/en/publications/frequent-trading-and-investment-performance-evidence-from-the-kos/)
- [REG] India, SEBI study (press release PR 37/2024, 23 Sep 2024): **93% of over 1 crore (10 million) individual equity F&O traders lost money FY22-FY24**, average loss ~Rs 2 lakh incl. costs; aggregate losses > Rs 1.8 lakh crore; only **1%** of individual traders earned profits above Rs 1 lakh after costs; >75% of loss-makers kept trading despite consecutive loss years; an earlier SEBI report (Jan 2023) found 89% lost in FY22. — [SEBI press release](https://www.sebi.gov.in/media-and-notifications/press-releases/sep-2024/updated-sebi-study-reveals-93-of-individual-traders-incurred-losses-in-equity-fando-between-fy22-and-fy24-aggregate-losses-exceed-1-8-lakh-crores-over-three-years_86906.html); [Taxmann summary](https://www.taxmann.com/post/blog/93-of-1-crore-fo-traders-saw-rs-2l-avg-loss-while-top-3-5-faced-rs-28l-avg-loss-incl-costs-sebi/)
- [PR] Coval, Hirshleifer, Shumway ("Can Individual Investors Beat the Market?", US discount broker 1990-1996, ~115k accounts; published Review of Asset Pricing Studies 2021): top-decile performers in the first half of the sample subsequently earn ~6%/yr risk-adjusted; WP version: their subsequent trades earn 15 bps/day excess, long-short (buy what prior winners buy, short what prior losers buy) 5 bps/day before costs; correlation of an individual's risk-adjusted performance across periods ~10%. — [Hirshleifer UCI PDF](https://sites.uci.edu/dhirshle/files/2019/06/Can-individual-investor-beat-the-market_0718_CHS.pdf); [WP PDF](https://econwpa.ub.uni-muenchen.de/econ-wp/fin/papers/0412/0412005.pdf); [U-Michigan news](https://news.umich.edu/many-investors-can-beat-the-market-contrary-to-popular-belief)
- [PR][WP] Heimer & Simsek, "Should Retail Investors' Leverage Be Limited?" (JFE): the 2010 US CFTC cap on retail FX leverage (50:1) vs unaffected EU traders (diff-in-diff): trading volume -23%, high-leverage traders' losses reduced by 40%, brokerages' operating capital -25%, bid-ask unaffected. Heimer (2014, Cleveland Fed WP 14-33): strong negative correlation between leverage used and per-trade returns; cap reduced per-trade losses even risk-adjusted. Heimer (2019 WP "Give 'em Enough Rope?"): cap alleviates up to three-quarters of per-trade losses; overconfident traders most affected. — [Harvard Law Forum summary](https://corpgov.law.harvard.edu/2019/06/13/should-retail-investors-leverage-be-limited); [SSRN 3095136](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=3095136); [Cleveland Fed WP 14-33](https://www.clevelandfed.org/publications/working-paper/2014/wp-1433-can-leverage-constraints-help-investors); [IDEAS WP 15-26](https://ideas.repec.org/p/fip/fedcwp/1526.html)
- [PR] Barber, Lee, Liu, Odean (2009, cited in the 2014 paper): Taiwan individual investors' aggregate trading losses exceed 2% of GDP (1995-1999). — [Barber et al. 2014 PDF, intro](https://faculty.haas.berkeley.edu/odean/papers/day%20traders/The%20Cross-Section%20of%20Speculator%20Skill.pdf)

### Inferences
- Loss rates cluster around 70-97% across countries, eras and instruments; the share *reliably* profitable is ~1% or less wherever it is measured (Taiwan <1%, Brazil 1.1% above minimum wage, India 1% above Rs 1 lakh).
- Heimer's results imply that, for a given (zero or negative) edge, higher leverage worsens per-trade returns — relevant to choosing prop risk per trade: overleveraging to hit a 10% target faster is the documented losing behaviour.
- Garvey & Murphy (2000-era US) is the only sizable sample with ~50% profitable, in a bull/bubble period with ECN quote-jumping opportunities that no longer exist.

### Gaps
- No Chinese individual day-trader profitability study was found in this pass (China's T+1 rule for A-shares limits stock day trading; futures evidence not located).
- Korean percentage of profitable individuals not found in abstracts.
- SEBI's separate intraday cash-segment study (often quoted as "~70% of intraday traders lost money", FY23) was not verified in this pass.

## Q4. Regulator and broker CFD disclosures

### Takeaway
EU regulators found 74-89% of retail CFD accounts lose money (2018); current mandatory broker warnings in the UK/EU are mostly ~65-80% losing accounts. These are account-level, all-instrument, after leverage caps.

### Cited Findings
- [REG] ESMA (27 Mar 2018): national competent authorities' analyses across EU jurisdictions showed **74-89% of retail CFD accounts typically lose money**, average losses per client **€1,600 to €29,000**; led to MiFIR Art. 40 product intervention: leverage caps 30:1 to 2:1 by underlying (indices 20:1, gold 20:1, major FX 30:1), 50% margin close-out per account, negative balance protection, ban on incentives, standardised risk warning. — [ESMA press release](https://www.esma.europa.eu/node/84933)
- [CO] Broker risk warnings (2026, self-reported over trailing 12 months): IG 78% of retail accounts lose (2.15% had positions closed due to margin call), Plus500 76%, Swissquote >81%, CMC 73% (BrokerChooser, May 2026); a TradingPlatforms report put IG and CMC at ~68%; mean across 14 FCA-authorised UK CFD brokers ~69.9% (April 2026). — [BrokerChooser CMC risk warning](https://brokerchooser.com/broker-reviews/cmc-markets-review/cfd-risk-warning); [Pound Sterling Live](https://www.poundsterlinglive.com/news/23132-even-at-major-uk-brokers-most-cfd-accounts-lost-money-report-finds); [The Investors Centre](https://www.theinvestorscentre.co.uk/trading/statistics/cfd-trading/)

### Inferences
- "Losing account %" over 12 months is a lower bound on the share without skill (some winners are lucky), consistent with Taiwan's 80% annual losers / <1% persistent winners split.

### Gaps
- Underlying NCA reports for the 2018 ESMA figure not retrieved; broker numbers vary by source/date and are not audited.

## Q5. Floor scalpers/locals (Silber 1984, Kuserk & Locke 1993, Manaster & Mann 1996) and whether that edge survives electronically

### Takeaway
Pit scalpers earned money mainly by supplying liquidity (capturing the bid-ask spread) and holding positions very briefly; profits fell as holding time rose. That edge was structural (floor access, being the counterparty to order flow) and has largely migrated to electronic market makers/HFTs; retail traders taking liquidity through a CFD broker are on the other side of it.

### Cited Findings
- [PR] Silber (1984), "Marketmaker Behavior in an Auction Market: An Analysis of Scalpers in Futures Markets", Journal of Finance 39(4), 937-953: using a representative scalper's transactions, scalpers provide liquidity services to incoming market orders (facilitating hedging); scalper earnings are positively related to the bid-ask spread and **negatively related to how long a position is held**. — [Journal of Finance issue listing](https://afajof.org/issue/volume-39-issue-4/); [IDEAS citation record](https://ideas.repec.org/r/bla/jfinan/v39y1984i4p937-53.html)
- [PR] Kuserk & Locke (1993), "Scalper behavior in futures markets: An empirical examination", Journal of Futures Markets 13(4), 409-431 (abstract not available online). — [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/wly/jfutmk/v13y1993i4p409-431.html)
- [PR] Manaster & Mann (1996), "Life in the Pits: Competitive Market Making and Inventory Control", Review of Financial Studies 9(3), 953-975: CME locals actively control inventory through the day, but inventory and reservation prices are *positively* correlated, contradicting standard inventory models and suggesting active position-taking (informed/skilled locals) rather than pure market making. Frino, Forrest & Duffy (1999) replicate with Sydney SPI futures. — [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/rfinst/v9y1996i3p953-75.html); [Macquarie record](https://researchers.mq.edu.au/en/publications/life-in-the-pits-competitive-market-making-and-inventory-control-/); [Frino et al. 1999](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/mulfin/v9y1999i3-4p373-385.html)
- [PR] In Taiwan's electronic limit-order market, retail day traders who relied on passive orders did not earn net profits (most-passive decile +3.8 bps/day gross, negative net). — [Barber et al. 2014](https://faculty.haas.berkeley.edu/odean/papers/day%20traders/The%20Cross-Section%20of%20Speculator%20Skill.pdf)
- [WP][2ND] Chague et al. conclude individuals cannot compete with HFTs in Brazilian mini-futures. — [Semantic Scholar](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Day-Trading-for-a-Living-Chague-De-Losso/15a142e2b9ec2c44d09c556b972274625c537d24)

### Inferences
- A CFD trader on FTMO's MT5 always pays the spread (no passive order queue at an exchange), so the scalper spread-capture edge is structurally unavailable; very short-hold strategies at FTMO face the full spread + commission on every trade.

### Gaps
- Specific Silber holding-time numbers (often quoted from memory as an average hold of ~2 minutes, with profits on positions held under ~3 minutes and losses on longer holds) could NOT be verified online; full text is behind JSTOR/Wiley. Treat as unverified.
- Kuserk & Locke and Manaster & Mann profit-per-contract numbers not retrieved.
- No source located in this pass quantifying modern electronic HFT market-maker holding times/profit (e.g. Kirilenko et al. flash-crash CFTC study) — out of scope time-wise.

## Q6. Prop-firm statistics and business model (FTMO, Topstep, The5ers, The Funded Trader, FPFX, FundedNext)

### Takeaway
No firm publishes audited pass rates. The best disclosure is Topstep's (2025): 16.8% of Combines passed, 33.3% of funded traders paid, 0.71% promoted to live. Industry back-end data (FPFX, ~300k accounts): ~7% of accounts ever get a payout, averaging ~4% of account size. FTMO's financials show the model is fee-funded: payouts were ~35% of revenue in 2023 and ~55% in 2024, and FTMO's net profit fell 59% in 2025 when a gold rally pushed payouts 82% above forecast.

### Cited Findings
- [CO] **Topstep 2025 (Jan-Dec)**: "16.8% of all Trading Combines initiated were successfully completed"; "51.8% of individual participants who entered one or more Trading Combines advanced to the Funded Level" (many buy multiple attempts); "33.3% of all individual participants at the Funded Level received a payout"; "0.71% of individual participants trading in an Express Funded Account were called up to a Live Funded Account". Statistics cover simulated and live environments. — [Topstep instant-payouts page disclosure](https://www.topstep.com/instant-payouts)
- [CO] Topstep: approves 99.26% of payout requests; traders keep 90% of profits. — [Topstep homepage/program pages via search](https://www.topstep.com/our-program)
- [PRESS][CO] The Funded Trader (CEO Angelo Ciaramello, Mar 2025): challenge pass rate 5-10%; ~20% of funded traders get a payout; overall 1-2 in 100 clients get paid. — [Finance Magnates, 18 Mar 2025](https://www.financemagnates.com/forex/only-1-in-20-traders-pass-prop-firm-challenges-reports-the-funded-trader/)
- [PRESS] FPFX Tech (back-office provider, 2024, ~300,000 prop accounts): **7% of accounts achieved a payout; payouts averaged 4% of funded account size** (e.g. $400 on $10k); average challenge cost $100. PipFarm survey: 41% of clients reach payout stage (outlier, self-selected). — [Finance Magnates, 18 Mar 2025](https://www.financemagnates.com/forex/only-1-in-20-traders-pass-prop-firm-challenges-reports-the-funded-trader/)
- [CO] FTMO does **not** publish a pass rate (none found on ftmo.com; third-party "8%/10-15%" figures are unverified). — [LuxAlgo/Forexadvocate-type reviews list estimates only](https://forexadvocate.com/ftmo-review-2026-rules-fees-payouts/); [FTMO 2021 in numbers (no pass rate)](https://ftmo.com/en/blog/year-2021-in-numbers/)
- [CO] FTMO 2021: >960,000 trading accounts created (Challenges, Verifications, FTMO Accounts, Free Trials); >90 million trades; $29.0M paid in 2021; **average payout per FTMO Account trading period $4,685**; 117.9 trades per FTMO Account trading period on average; most traded instrument **XAUUSD**, then EURUSD, GBPUSD, GBPJPY...; indices US30, US100. — [FTMO blog "The year 2021 in numbers"](https://ftmo.com/en/blog/year-2021-in-numbers/)

### Inferences
- Rough FTMO-like funnel from the best available data: per-attempt pass ~10-17%; of funded, ~20-33% ever paid; per-attempt chance of any payout ~5-7%. Average payout ≈ 6% of nominal account per paid trading period (FTMO table), i.e. ~€6k on a $100k account vs a ~€540 fee — so a *random* trader's EV per attempt is negative (≈0.06 × 6,000 ≈ €360 < fee), which is exactly how the firm earns.
- FTMO payouts/revenue 35-55% and 2025's profit collapse on the gold trend show that FTMO's P&L is effectively short its traders' aggregate P&L: if many traders had a real edge, the model would break. This is consistent with academic loss rates.
- Topstep's 51.8% "per person" vs 16.8% "per attempt" gap shows serial re-buying; 0.71% reaching live capital mirrors the <1% persistent-skill share in Taiwan.

### Gaps
- No official FTMO pass-rate or payout-rate statistic exists publicly; all FTMO pass-rate numbers seen (5-15%, "8%", "7% of passers paid") are unverified third-party claims.
- No dataset found on *what passing prop traders do differently* (risk per trade, holding time, instruments) beyond FTMO's anecdotal interviews (n=3: e.g. 0.13%-1% risk per trade, single-instrument focus, session-specific entries — [FTMO blog](https://ftmo.com/en/blog/how-do-our-best-ftmo-traders-trade/)) and FTMO's guidance of 0.5-1.5% risk per trade ([FTMO blog](https://ftmo.com/en/blog/how-much-should-you-risk-on-one-trade/)). These are marketing, not data.
- FundedNext pass/payout rates not found.

## Q7. Common characteristics of the small profitable minority

### Takeaway
Across datasets, the profitable minority is identified best by a long, statistically strong past record; secondarily by specialization (few instruments), higher activity (more experience/volume), somewhat more use of passive orders, trading where information is uneven, and disciplined loss-cutting/low leverage. None of these traits on their own produces profitability.

### Cited Findings
- [PR] Past performance is "by a large margin" the best predictor; then concentration in a few stocks; profitable traders lean slightly more on passive orders (64% vs 70-78% aggressive) but still mostly take liquidity; more active traders have higher gross alpha but still lose net. — [Barber et al. 2014](https://faculty.haas.berkeley.edu/odean/papers/day%20traders/The%20Cross-Section%20of%20Speculator%20Skill.pdf)
- [WP] Profitable traders' returns increase with experience; those with >40 day-trading days in the prior year cover 40 bps round-trip costs, with profit earned mostly on the trade day. — [Barber et al. learning WP 2017](https://faculty.haas.berkeley.edu/odean/papers/day%20traders/day%20trading%20and%20learning%20110217.pdf)
- [PR] In Taiwan futures, excessive trading hurts overconfident losers but not winners; experience increases aggressiveness without skill. — [Kuo & Lin 2013](https://hub.hku.hk/bitstream/10722/184789/1/content.pdf)
- [WP] Profitable US prop day traders are more active when uninformed/liquidity traders are present and improve quotes ahead of market makers. — [Garvey & Murphy abstract](https://content.csbs.utah.edu/~ehrbar/erc2002/abstracts/A407.html)
- [PR] Faster loss realization would have improved professional day traders' profits (disposition effect is costly). — [Garvey & Murphy 2004 FAJ](https://rpc.cfainstitute.org/research/financial-analysts-journal/2004/are-professional-traders-too-slow-to-realize-their-losses)
- [PR] Higher leverage is associated with worse per-trade returns. — [Heimer 2014 Cleveland Fed WP](https://www.clevelandfed.org/publications/working-paper/2014/wp-1433-can-leverage-constraints-help-investors)
- [PR] Scalper (market-maker) profits rise with the spread and fall with holding time. — [Silber 1984 via JF issue](https://afajof.org/issue/volume-39-issue-4/)

### Inferences
- For a systematic CFD bot: the realistic "winner traits" that transfer are (1) a verified, statistically significant track record before scaling (mirrors "past Sharpe is the best predictor"), (2) specialization in few instruments/sessions, (3) low leverage / small risk per trade, (4) mechanical loss-cutting. The information-asymmetry and liquidity-provision channels documented for stock day traders and floor scalpers are not available to a retail MT5 CFD account.
- The base rate for any new strategy/trader is very poor (≤1% persistent net skill). This supports the project's strict out-of-sample/deflated-Sharpe protocol: a pass of one IS window is the equivalent of the ~20% "profitable this year" group, not the <1% skilled group.

### Gaps
- No study located that measures holding period vs profitability for retail *index CFD* or *gold* intraday traders specifically.
- No public cross-sectional data on FTMO traders' instruments/holding times/risk per trade split by pass/fail.
