# Proven Individual Traders: Verified Records vs. Marketing (competition winners, Market Wizards, intraday educators)

Verification labels used throughout:
- **[AUDITED/INSTITUTIONAL]** = large outside-money fund whose returns are reported by press or databases from investor documents (not a public audit we could inspect, but institutional money with auditors and investors on the other side).
- **[COMPETITION-VERIFIED]** = checked by a contest organiser against brokerage/clearing statements. That makes it a verified *leaderboard*. It is not an audited composite.
- **[SELF-REPORTED]** = trader's own website, book, interview or marketing.
- **[SECONDARY/UNVERIFIED]** = forum, blog or compilation that we could not trace to a primary document.

---

## 1. Trading competitions (Robbins World Cup, US Investing Championship): verification, rules, winners, lottery effects, persistence

### Takeaway
Both contests use real money and check results against broker statements, so the headline numbers are probably real. But they are the right tail of a self-selected, highly leveraged field, and most entrants lose. On the US Investing Championship (USIC) only 8% (2022) to 17% (H1 2026) of entrants reported a profit. Winners' peak returns are often far above their final ones, and some interim leaders disappear from the board. The contest format rewards maximum variance, which is the opposite of what FTMO's 5% daily / 10% max-loss rules reward. Few winners went on to show audited, allocatable money-management records. The best-documented case, Larry Williams, has a 1987 NFA matter: reported first-quarter-1987 managed-account losses of about $6.1M while his personal account gained (this rests on secondary sources).

**Lottery/tournament effects (academic)**
- In contest experiments, risk-taking rises with the number of competitors and with how sensitive winning is to performance, with a significant interaction between the two (Spadoni & Potters, *Games* 2018). — [Tilburg repository](https://research.tilburguniversity.edu/en/publications/the-effect-of-competition-on-risk-taking-in-contests/)
- Competitiveness drives excess risk-taking. Adding contestants and giving feedback on winners' choices both increased it, and in the most competitive treatment fewer than 10% played the optimal no-risk strategy early on (Eriksen & Kvaløy 2015). — [UiS working paper](https://www.ba-odegaard.no/uis_wps_econ_fin/uis_wps_2015_01_eriksen_kvaloy.pdf)
- Tournament theory predicts that winner-take-all contestants choose maximal risk. Experiments support this only partly: leaders "play it safe" and trailers gamble. — [Bonn BGSE DP 7/2008 (Nieken)](https://www.wiwi.uni-bonn.de/bgsepapers/old%20series/bonedp/bgse7_2008.pdf)
- No academic study directly testing the persistence of Robbins/USIC winners was found (see Gaps).

### Inferences
- Contest returns are probably genuine, since real money is checked against statements. They are still not evidence of a repeatable edge. Consider a few hundred to a thousand-plus leveraged entrants per year: with zero edge, some still compound to +500–1,000% by chance. A "winner" may simply be the luckiest draw from a high-variance field, and losers are invisible because monthly listing is voluntary. The organiser's own 8%/17% profitable shares confirm the field is mostly losers.
- The incentive structure is the opposite of prop-firm rules. A contest pays only for the top of the distribution, so the best response is to maximise variance; Larry Williams said leverage could have taken him to 20,000%. A Gajjala-style −44% month, or the drop from +1,358% to +970%, would breach FTMO's 5% daily or 10% max-loss limit in a few days. Contest-style sizing is directly disqualifying under FTMO.
- Repeat winners (Unger 4×, Davey 3 straight podiums, Sean Ryan 7/7, J Law 2024–25) are much stronger evidence than single spikes. Even so, none published an audited, allocatable composite that we could find. Several monetise through courses or subscriptions (Unger Academy, Davey's books/courses, Minervini Private Access), so contest titles also work as marketing.
- The sponsors have conflicts of interest. Robbins is a brokerage that earns commissions from leveraged entrants and was itself caught up in the Williams NFA matter. Zadeh used USIC results to solicit investors for unregistered funds.

### Gaps
- Robbins World Cup entrant counts per year, the current entry fee (the $3,999/year label is ambiguous) and the verification procedure (clearing-firm statements?) are not documented on the official page. The official full champions list could not be retrieved (past-results URL returned 404).
- No primary NFA document was retrieved for the Williams 1987 matter. The $6.12M / $902,599 / $13,000 figures come from forum quotes of a book.
- No audited, allocatable track record was found for Unger, Davey, Minervini or Michelle Williams after their wins. Minervini's later public money management, if any, is unverified.
- No peer-reviewed study of trading-contest winners' later performance was found.

## 2. Market Wizards and famous traders: method, timeframe, verified vs self-reported performance

### Takeaway
The strongest records belong to traders who later ran large outside-money funds: Paul Tudor Jones (Tudor BVI), Bruce Kovner (Caxton) and Jim Simons (Medallion). Their published long-run figures (about 18–22%/yr net for PTJ and Kovner, and about 39% net for Medallion, possibly overstated) are far below the headline returns of contest winners, and they rely on scale, information, infrastructure and diversified macro or quant processes, not short-term chart patterns. The famous retail-facing stories are mostly self-reported or secondhand: Seykota's $5k → $12–15M "model account", the Turtles' "$175M" and "80%/yr", and Raschke's "17th of 4,500". Richard Dennis's own record ends in heavy client losses (1987–88), a settlement of over $2.5M and fund closures in 2000.

### Cited Findings

**Paul Tudor Jones (Tudor Investment Corp.)**: discretionary global macro, technically driven
- The Tudor fund returned **+125.9% after fees in 1987** (positioned for the crash) and **+87.4% in 1990** (short Japan). His style is global macro relying mainly on technical analysis and momentum rather than value. His fees are 4% management and 23% performance. [Wikipedia, press-derived] — [Wikipedia: Paul Tudor Jones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Tudor_Jones)
- The flagship Tudor BVI Global Fund averaged about **18%/yr since 1986** (as of 2016) and was down 2.5% YTD in July 2016. [AUDITED/INSTITUTIONAL, press-reported] — [Fortune 2016](https://fortune.com/2016/08/17/this-billionaires-hedge-fund-is-said-to-be-laying-off-15-of-staff)
- An earlier figure was 22.1% annualised from Oct 1986 to June 2009, so the post-2009 years were weaker. — [Opalesque 2009 ranking](https://www.opalesque.com/OFI189/Top_have_a_surprise_for_you_the89.html)
- In 2017 the fund was −1.2% YTD (June) versus S&P +9%. — [CNBC 2017](https://www.cnbc.com/2017/06/13/hedge-fund-legend-paul-tudor-jones-main-fund-is-down-this-year.html)

**Bruce Kovner (Caxton Associates, 1983–2011)**: discretionary global macro (FX, rates, commodities)
- Averaged about **21%/yr after fees** over 1983–2011 (Bloomberg's calculation). He retired in 2011 and handed the firm to Andrew Law. [AUDITED/INSTITUTIONAL, press-calculated] — [Bloomberg Billionaires](https://www.bloomberg.com/billionaires/profiles/bruce-s-kovner/); [Institutional Investor](https://www.institutionalinvestor.com/article/b18bkfvtb4tfp9/kovner-retires-as-caxton-faces-an-uncertain-future); [Motley Fool 2013, about 20%](https://www.fool.com/investing/general/2013/12/18/heres-what-this-annual-20-gainer-has-been-buying.aspx)

**Jim Simons / Renaissance Medallion**: fully systematic, short-horizon statistical models, closed to outsiders

**Linda Bradford Raschke**: short-term discretionary/pattern futures trader (New Market Wizards)

**Marty Schwartz**: S&P futures/stock index day/short-term trader (Market Wizards; *Pit Bull*)
- His contest record (3rd in 1983, +69.2% in the Aug–Dec 1983 futures contest, "widely reported" +781% in 1984) rests on his own book and later compilations (grade C/D). — [CTE sheet](https://completetradersedge.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/CTE-Research-Sheet-US-Investing-Championship-2.pdf)

### Inferences
- The track records are strongest where outside institutional money and auditors were involved (PTJ, Kovner, Simons). They show durable but modest compounding (about 18–22%/yr net for the discretionary macro managers), long drawdown and underperformance stretches (PTJ in 2016–17), and decay over time (PTJ's 22.1% to 2009 vs 18% to 2016).
- Retail-legend stories (Seykota's model account, the Turtles' $175M, Faith's 80–100%/yr, Raschke's ranking) cannot be checked against NFA/BarclayHedge composites in public sources. Treat them as self-reported.
- Trend following (Dennis, Turtles, Seykota, Parker) is the one style where the method is public and the results were partly verified. It also shows the failure mode: Wikipedia cites backtests that are flat from 1996 to 2009, and Dennis blew up when he deviated from risk discipline. Edge decay after publication or crowding is a recurring theme.
- The method lineage of the verified winners is mostly multi-day to multi-month trend/macro, or very high-frequency quant with large infrastructure (Medallion). Verified *retail intraday* discretionary success at scale is scarce in this group.

### Gaps
- BarclayHedge / NFA BASIC year-by-year composites for Chesapeake (Parker), Raschke's fund, Dennis's 1990s funds and Larry Williams were not retrieved. NFA BASIC is the place to check registration and disciplinary history.
- PTJ's widely quoted rules (e.g., 5:1 reward/risk, exiting below the 200-day moving average, "defense first") come from interviews (Schwager, Tony Robbins) that were not fetched in this session.
- Kovner's documented risk rules from Schwager's *Market Wizards* (e.g., risking under 1% per trade, placing stops at technically meaningful levels) were not re-verified here. They are recalled from the book and should be checked before quoting.
- No year-by-year Tudor BVI or Caxton returns were obtained; the averages are press figures.

## 3. Intraday / orderflow traders and the education business: is there verified evidence?

### Takeaway
No independent, audited performance evidence was found for intraday or orderflow educator-traders (SMB Capital, Axia Futures and similar). What exists is self-published statistics, sales funnels and mixed customer reviews. The only large-sample, independent evidence on intraday traders is academic. In Taiwan, fewer than 1% of day traders (about 1,000 of about 360,000 per year) predictably outperform after costs, and in Brazil about 97% of persistent day traders lose money. Persistent intraday skill exists but is extremely rare. The few who have it earn large per-trade alphas taken from less-skilled retail counterparties.

### Cited Findings
- **Barber, Lee, Liu & Odean, "The Cross-Section of Speculator Skill" (Taiwan, complete TSE data, 1992–2006; *J. Financial Markets* 2014)**: about 360,000 individuals day trade in an average year. About 13–15% earn profits net of fees in a typical year, but "**less than 1% of day traders (1,000 out of 360,000) are able to outperform consistently**". The top 500 from the prior year earn 49.5 bps/day gross and **28.1 bps/day net** on their day-trading portfolio, but only about **2.0 bps/day alpha on their total portfolio (over 5%/yr)**. Traders with a prior loss history go on to earn −17.5 bps/day gross and −34.2 bps/day net. The top 500 had a mean Sharpe ratio of 0.21 in the ranking period (frequency not stated in the passage read, plausibly daily). Counterparties to the profitable trades are less-active day traders and individual investors. — [Berkeley working paper PDF](https://faculty.haas.berkeley.edu/odean/papers/day%20traders/Day%20Trading%20Skill%20110523.pdf)
- **Barber, Lee, Liu, Odean & Zhang, "Learning, Fast or Slow" (*Review of Asset Pricing Studies* 2020)**: aggregate day-trader performance is negative. **74% of day-trading volume comes from traders with a history of losses, and 97% of day traders are likely to lose money in future day trading.** — [IDEAS/RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/rasset/v10y2020i1p61-93..html)
- **Chague, De-Losso & Giovannetti, "Day Trading for a Living?" (Brazil, futures)**: of those who day-traded more than 300 days, about 97% lost money and only about 1% earned more than the Brazilian minimum wage. [cited via secondary summary] — [UMich MJE summary](https://sites.lsa.umich.edu/mje/2026/05/11/clicks-to-cash-debunking-the-myth-of-day-trading-profitability/); [CXO Advisory](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/individual-investing/do-day-traders-make-money/)
- **SMB Capital**: a NYC prop desk plus a training business. Its co-founder says on SMB's own blog that it "puts statistics in front of everybody" (self-published). Reviews call its seminars "hard sell conversion funnels" and a program "a very expensive lesson in buyer's remorse", while others praise the training. No independent audited trader-outcome data was found. — [SMB blog](https://www.smbtraining.com/blog/17-questions-for-proprietary-trading-firm-co-founder-brutally-honest-answers); [Trustpilot SMB](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/smbtraining.com); [EliteTrader thread](https://www.elitetrader.com/et/threads/smb-u-options-training.335081/post-4908893)
- **Axia Futures (London)**: describes itself as a trading education firm whose trading team is cleared and risk-managed by FCA-regulated AXIA Markets Pro (self-description). A reviewer says the program costs more than expected because progression depends on continued paid participation. No independent performance data for its traders was found. — [Trustpilot Axia](https://dk.trustpilot.com/review/axiafutures.com)

### Inferences
- The business model of orderflow/intraday educators (courses, mentorships, "funded" pathways, signal rooms) earns revenue from the large population of losing aspirants that the Taiwan and Brazil data describe. The incentive is to show winners, not denominators. Without audited, full-population outcomes, their claims are marketing.
- The Taiwan top-500 result is real evidence of intraday skill. But that skill is (a) extremely rare, (b) mainly a stock-picking or liquidity edge against uninformed retail in a single-stock market with retail counterparties, and (c) small at the total-portfolio level (about 5%/yr alpha). Index CFDs and gold on a market maker's feed have no comparable identifiable "dumb" counterparty flow.
- The top Taiwan traders were somewhat less aggressive (71% of trades from aggressive orders vs 71–75% for other day traders): paying the spread less helps. — [Berkeley paper](https://faculty.haas.berkeley.edu/odean/papers/day%20traders/Day%20Trading%20Skill%20110523.pdf)

### Gaps
- No audited or regulator-sourced outcome data for SMB, Axia, Jigsaw or similar orderflow educators was found. Chicago "locals" (pit scalpers) were profitable mainly through exchange-floor structural advantages (seat ownership, low fees, seeing order flow). No source was retrieved to quantify this.
- The original Chague et al. paper was not fetched; the 97%/1% figures are from secondary summaries.

## 4. Common traits of the verified winners

### Takeaway
The verifiable winners share four things: strict loss control with survival first (the blowups, Dennis in 1987–88 and 2000, came from rule deviation and outsized risk); a process that is either diversified trend/macro over days to months, or industrial-scale quant; long careers with multi-year drawdowns and decaying returns; and often an edge in information, structure or counterparty. None of the institutional-grade records rests on classic retail chart patterns traded intraday on a single CFD.

### Cited Findings
- Long-run net returns for verified institutional winners cluster around **18–22%/yr** (PTJ about 18% since 1986, Kovner about 21% for 1983–2011), with an outlier quant firm at about **39% net** (Medallion, contested). — [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2016/08/17/this-billionaires-hedge-fund-is-said-to-be-laying-off-15-of-staff); [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/billionaires/profiles/bruce-s-kovner/); [CXO](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/investing-expertise/proof-of-superior-investment-expertise/)
- The big years were rare macro events (PTJ +125.9% in 1987, +87.4% in 1990) and were followed by long ordinary or flat periods (−2.5% and −1.2% YTD readings in 2016–17). — [Wikipedia PTJ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Tudor_Jones); [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2017/06/13/hedge-fund-legend-paul-tudor-jones-main-fund-is-down-this-year.html)
- Failures came from breaking rules: Dennis's 1990 settlement was over "failure to follow his own rules", and Parker's 2006–07 deviations underperformed. — [Wikipedia Dennis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Dennis); [Advisor Perspectives](https://www.advisorperspectives.com/commentaries/2023/05/01/charting-my-interruption-cmi-mind-over-markets-the-importance-of-proper-mindset)
- Edges decay: Turtle-system backtests show a drastic drop after 1986 and a flat 1996–2009. — [Wikipedia Dennis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Dennis)
- Persistence, not single spikes, is the skill signal. Even in a verified contest, the path shows large intra-year drawdowns (a −44.4% month for a 2023 winner). — [CTE sheet](https://completetradersedge.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/CTE-Research-Sheet-US-Investing-Championship-2.pdf)
- Persistent intraday skill exists for less than 1% of traders and comes from trading against less-informed retail flow. — [Barber et al.](https://faculty.haas.berkeley.edu/odean/papers/day%20traders/Day%20Trading%20Skill%20110523.pdf)

### Inferences
- Trend-following and macro winners have positive skew: many small losses and a few huge winners (1987, 1990). That profile clashes with FTMO's daily-loss rule and its 10%-target-within-unlimited-time structure only mildly. It clashes much more with the need to avoid large give-backs, because a trend system's typical drawdowns of 20–30% far exceed FTMO's 10% max loss.
- Specialisation varies. Discretionary macro traders were diversified across asset classes; contest winners and Taiwan top day traders specialise. The common denominator is risk control and a genuine edge, not a particular pattern or market.
- Capital and leverage: contest records come from small accounts ($10k–$250k) at extreme leverage, while institutional records come from billions at modest leverage. The prop-firm setting (a $100k–$200k account with 5%/10% loss caps) sits structurally closer to the institutional risk profile than to contests.

### Gaps
- No systematic dataset links Schwager interviewees to verified CTA composites. A precise "X of N Market Wizards have audited records" figure could not be produced.
- Specific per-trade risk numbers (e.g., Kovner under 1%, PTJ 5:1) were not re-verified in this session.

## 5. What is realistically transferable to an automated intraday approach on index CFDs and gold (FTMO rules)

### Takeaway
What carries over is the risk-management discipline (small fixed fractional risk, hard stops, no deviation from rules, survival over upside), and the evidence standard: persistence across years and out of sample, full denominators, and suspicion of single extreme results. What does not carry over is contest-style leverage, the multi-month trend/macro horizons and diversification that produced the verified macro and trend records, the counterparty or information edges of Taiwan top day traders or Medallion, and the marketing claims of intraday educators.

### Cited Findings
- Contest winners' paths include −44% months and gains reversed by hundreds of points within a year, which is incompatible with FTMO's 5% daily / 10% max loss. — [CTE sheet](https://completetradersedge.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/CTE-Research-Sheet-US-Investing-Championship-2.pdf)
- Even verified intraday skill (Taiwan top 500) is about 2 bps/day at the total-portfolio level, and fewer than 1 in 360 traders achieve it. — [Barber et al.](https://faculty.haas.berkeley.edu/odean/papers/day%20traders/Day%20Trading%20Skill%20110523.pdf)
- Systematic contest traders do exist: Kevin Davey placed in the top two for three years running with algorithmic systems and wrote a book on building algorithmic systems. — [CTE](https://completetradersedge.com/?p=235275); [Wiley](https://wiley-vch.e-bookshelf.de/building-winning-algorithmic-trading-systems-website-2633092.html)

### Inferences
- Use contest results only as proof that real-money returns can be extreme in the tail, never as a strategy benchmark. The project's existing protocol (pre-registration, IS/OOS split, locked 2024+, deflated Sharpe/PBO, a trial registry) is exactly the filter that separates Section 1 "winners" from skill.
- A realistic target for a verified systematic edge is in the institutional range (tens of % per year at most, with multi-month flat periods). It is not the contest range. FTMO economics should be modelled with that in mind.
- The traits worth copying are: fixed small risk per trade, a pre-defined daily stop (mirroring FTMO's daily loss), no discretionary overrides (the Dennis and Parker lessons), and monitoring for edge decay (Turtle post-1986).
- The edges that verified winners used and a retail intraday bot lacks are information/flow (Taiwan top traders vs retail; Medallion's infrastructure), macro event exposure (PTJ 1987/1990) and multi-asset diversification. Any intraday index/gold edge must come from documented structural effects (the project's own research list), and the evidence so far suggests these are small after costs.

### Gaps
- No verified example was found of an individual who passed and repeatedly earned payouts at FTMO-type firms with an automated intraday index/gold system and published audited results. Prop-firm payout statistics are outside this note's scope.
